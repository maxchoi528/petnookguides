label math

Dry matter basis:comparing wetvs dry labels.

Wet foods often look “lower” in protein or fat on the guaranteed analysis simply because they carry much more water. Dry matter basis math removes the water so you can compare nutrient percentages at a near-equal moisture level.

Educational only. Cats and dogs with medical diet needs (kidney disease, pancreatitis, urinary disease, growth problems, food allergy workups, diabetes, or unexplained weight loss) should use a veterinarian’s guidance rather than label math alone.

A quick way to compare wet and dry foods without letting water content distort the label percentages.

The short version

If you are comparing a canned food to a dry food, convert the protein and fat numbers to a dry matter basis first. FDA explains the conversion: dry matter % = 100 − moisture %, then dry-matter nutrient % = (as-fed % ÷ dry matter %) × 100.

Method: what dry matter basis is (and isn’t)

Dry matter basis is a moisture-free way to express nutrient percentages. It is useful for comparing nutrient percentages between products with very different moisture content, such as canned and dry foods.

It is not a shortcut for deciding how much to feed. Portion planning still comes back to calories (kcal) and how your pet’s body condition responds over time.

What the label says: guaranteed analysis is “as fed” and usually min/max

AAFCO notes that proximate analysis results are typically reported as “as fed,” meaning the water is included in the number. Tufts also emphasizes a second limitation: the guaranteed analysis does not usually give the actual nutrient percentage in the food. Protein and fat are listed as minimums, while fiber and moisture are listed as maximums, and the true value can vary.

Dry matter conversion is therefore best used as a comparison tool, not a precision measurement.

What to calculate: dry matter % and then the converted nutrient %

  1. Find the moisture guarantee (%) in the guaranteed analysis.
  2. Compute dry matter % = 100 − moisture %.
  3. For each nutrient you want to compare, compute: (as-fed % ÷ dry matter %) × 100.

Example (protein): if the label shows minimum protein 12% and maximum moisture 78%, the dry matter is 22% (100 − 78), and protein on a dry matter basis is about 54.5% (12 ÷ 22 × 100). FDA uses this same structure to show why a dry food can look “higher” on the label but end up lower once moisture is removed.

For fiber and moisture, remember you’re working with maximums. For protein and fat, you’re working with minimums.

Product comparison notes: what this helps you see

  • Some wet foods are not “low protein” once you remove the water.
  • Two canned foods with the same moisture guarantee can be compared directly without conversion.
  • Dry matter basis compares nutrient percentages, not calories per can, cup, or ounce.

If your goal is weight control, meal planning still works best from calories per serving and gradual body-condition changes, not from protein % alone.

Common mistakes

  • Comparing canned vs dry without converting for moisture.
  • Treating minimums and maximums as exact values.
  • Using dry matter basis to decide portion size without checking kcal.
  • Assuming a higher % automatically means a better food for every cat or dog.

When to ask a veterinarian

Use veterinary guidance when the question involves kittens, puppies, pregnancy/lactation, unexplained weight loss, chronic vomiting/diarrhea, diabetes, kidney disease, pancreatitis, urinary disease, or any prescription or elimination-diet plan.
